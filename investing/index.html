01

The thesis

Every trend that's supposed to define the next thirty years (AI, electrified transport, automation, the grid itself) runs on the same substrate: silicon. I don't know which company wins any given cycle, and I don't need to. The sector as a whole is upstream of nearly everything, which makes it less a stock pick and more a bet that computation keeps mattering.

That's also why I hold it as a sector, not as single names. Semiconductors are brutally cyclical (inventory gluts, export controls, fab delays), and individual companies get crushed by cycles the industry shrugs off. An index of the sector keeps the thesis and diversifies away the single-company ways to be right about the future and still lose money.

02

The vehicles

Broad index funds are the base of the portfolio, the boring majority. The semiconductor thesis sits on top as a deliberate tilt, held through ETFs on both sides of the border.

03

The horizon

The run log on this page is the strategy. In year one, contributions are everything and returns are noise. Somewhere past year ten, that inverts: the compounding on old contributions starts outweighing anything new money can do. The entire design of the portfolio is to survive long enough, financially and emotionally, to reach that inversion.

Volatility is the admission price, not a malfunction. A semiconductor tilt will have gut-punch years; the plan accounts for them by never holding anything I'd be forced to sell on a bad day. Same discipline as the dough and the nightly jobs: set the process up correctly, then let it run without interference.
